Your Dispatcher Should Not Have to Solve the Same Puzzle Every Day

Every morning—or every evening—someone in your business has to decide:

  • Which vehicle should handle each delivery?
  • In what order should the stops be made?
  • Can everything fit within the available vehicle capacity?
  • Which customers must be visited within a specific time window?
  • How can drivers avoid unnecessary travel?
  • What should change when a new order arrives or a vehicle becomes unavailable?

An experienced dispatcher may be able to manage this using spreadsheets and personal knowledge.

But the process can become increasingly difficult as the operation grows.

Planning may take hours. Routes may depend heavily on one person’s experience. Small changes can require the entire plan to be rearranged. Vehicles may travel farther than necessary, while some drivers or trucks are overloaded and others are underused.

A customized route-planning tool helps your team consider these factors together—quickly, consistently, and with less manual effort.

From Delivery Spreadsheet to Practical Daily Routes

The process begins with information you may already have.

This could include:

  • Customer names and delivery locations
  • Orders scheduled for the day
  • Product quantities or shipment sizes
  • Available vehicles
  • Vehicle capacities
  • Driver working hours
  • Customer delivery windows
  • Loading and unloading times
  • Priority customers
  • Collections or return trips
  • Special vehicle or access requirements

You do not need to begin with a sophisticated transport-management system.

Existing spreadsheets, order lists, and practical knowledge from your dispatch team can provide the starting point.

The resulting interactive planning tool is configured around your operation, allowing your team to generate, review, and adjust daily routes more efficiently.

How It Works

  1. 1. Provide the Day’s Deliveries

    Enter or import the orders, deliveries, or collections that need to be completed.

    This can be based on the spreadsheets and order formats your team already uses.

  2. 2. Apply Your Operating Requirements

    The planning model considers the factors that matter to your business, such as vehicle capacity, delivery times, driver availability, customer priorities, and route restrictions.

  3. 3. Generate Practical Routes

    The tool evaluates possible combinations and produces a practical route plan for the available vehicles and drivers.

  4. 4. Review and Adjust

    Your dispatcher remains in control.

    Routes can be reviewed and adjusted when local knowledge, customer requests, or unexpected circumstances require a different decision.

The objective is not to replace your team’s judgement. It is to give them a stronger and faster starting point.

Built Around Your Operation—not a Generic Template

Every delivery business works differently.

A food distributor may need to account for refrigerated vehicles and strict delivery times.

A supermarket chain may need to replenish multiple stores from one or more distribution points.

A building-material supplier may need to consider vehicle size, unloading time, and site-access limitations.

A regional logistics company may need to manage different customers, vehicle types, drivers, collections, and recurring delivery commitments.

Generic route-planning software may work well when the operation fits its standard assumptions.

But many established businesses have processes, customer requirements, and practical constraints that do not fit neatly into an off-the-shelf system.

A customized planning tool can be designed around factors such as:

  • Different vehicle types and capacities
  • Multiple depots or loading points
  • Fixed or flexible delivery windows
  • Driver shift limits
  • Customer priorities
  • Recurring delivery patterns
  • Preferred delivery sequences
  • Loading and unloading times
  • Vehicle-access restrictions
  • Return collections
  • Specialist or refrigerated vehicles
  • Manual dispatcher overrides
  • Business-specific operating rules

The tool adapts to the way your business operates, rather than forcing your operation into a rigid standard workflow.

The goal is not to introduce complexity into your business.

The goal is to place sophisticated analysis behind a planning tool that is straightforward for your team to use.

Depending on the operation, the solution may draw on:

  • Route optimization
  • Operations research
  • Statistical modelling

  • Machine learning
  • Custom software development

The method is selected according to the problem. Technology is used where it produces a practical operational benefit—not simply for its own sake.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do we need to replace our existing order-management system?

Not necessarily.

The route-planning tool can begin with the information your team already maintains, including spreadsheets and exported order lists. Any integration requirements can be discussed based on your current systems and preferred workflow.

Do we need GPS or vehicle-tracking data?

Not necessarily.

GPS or telematics data may be useful for some projects, but the initial planning model can often begin with delivery locations, orders, vehicle details, operating constraints, and knowledge from your dispatch team.

Will the tool make all decisions automatically?

The tool is intended to support your dispatcher, not remove their control.

It can generate a strong initial route plan while allowing experienced staff to review and adjust the result based on circumstances that may not be fully represented in the data.

Is this the same as a standard route-planning subscription?

No.

The objective is to create an interactive planning tool configured around your delivery operation and its specific requirements.

This is most valuable when spreadsheets have become difficult to manage and generic software does not adequately reflect the way your business works.

Is the solution only for large fleets?

No.

Suitability depends less on the number of vehicles than on the complexity and value of the routing problem.

A business with a modest fleet may still benefit if it manages many daily stops, strict delivery windows, capacity limitations, multiple vehicle types, or complicated customer requirements.

Can the tool accommodate last-minute changes?

The specific functionality will depend on the agreed project scope, but the planning approach can be designed to help your team update routes when orders, vehicles, or operating conditions change.
